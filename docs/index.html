The method

A state machine, not a chatbot.

Onboarding is a funnel with five verified steps. Deterministic code owns progression — the conversation can never skip a step, guess a balance, or improvise with money. The assistant talks; the machine decides.

  1. 01

    Greeting

    The anti-scam pledge comes before anything else: no money, no seed words, no documents, ever — and how to reach a human. Trust is the first deliverable. exit: pledge delivered

  2. 02

    Wallet

    Install, sign up, choose a username — one instruction per message, at the newcomer's pace. The wallet is then verified against the live Lightning network, not taken on faith. exit: wallet verified

  3. 03

    First receive

    A community member sends the newcomer their first sats, and Coral confirms what they saw happen. This is the moment Bitcoin stops being an idea. exit: receipt confirmed

  4. 04

    First spend

    Coral issues a small test invoice and verifies settlement cryptographically. The newcomer has now sent a real payment — provably, not anecdotally. exit: settlement verified

  5. 05

    Community

    The end state is not an app install — it's a person who knows people. A group invite, an introduction, a next meetup, and a 30-day check-in. exit: joined

Escalation is a feature, not a failure. A request for a human, a safety trigger, frustration, or three failed verifications pauses the funnel and pings a real community member with full context. When it's resolved, Coral resumes exactly where it left off.

Proof, not promises

Verified at every step. Zero credentials.

Coral verifies progress against the live network using open Lightning standards — it holds no funds, holds no keys, and needs no API access to anyone's wallet.

ClaimHow Coral knows
The wallet exists Every username is a Lightning address. Coral resolves it against the live network before moving on. LNURL-pay lookup · no keys required
A payment arrived Confirmed in conversation with the newcomer — amount and notification, not just “ok”. An automated welcome payment is available where the community enables it. guided confirmation · optional faucet
A payment was sent Coral issues a small test invoice and polls its settlement proof. Either the payment settled or it didn't — no self-reporting. LUD-21 verify · cryptographic settlement
Anything Coral did Every action appends to an immutable event log. “Why did Coral do that?” always has an exact, timestamped answer. append-only audit trail

Coral cannot ask for what it cannot use — it has nowhere to put your money and no use for your keys.

The hard rules

Non-negotiables, enforced in code.

Newcomers are the softest target in Bitcoin. Coral's safety posture is not a policy document — it's the part of the system that cannot be talked out of anything.

Rule 1

Never asks. Never accepts.

Seed phrases, private keys, passwords, documents, money — Coral will not request them and will not take them, and it says so in its first message.

Rule 2

Seed words trip an alarm.

Any mention of recovery phrases — from anyone, in any direction — triggers an immediate warning and a human follow-up. Unconditionally.

Rule 3

“Human” always works.

One word reaches a real community member, any time, no argument. Coral is also honest about being software, every time it's asked.

Rule 4

Invited, not inbound.

Coral meets people through the community — a meetup, a referral, a trusted introduction. It does no cold outreach, ever.

For operators

Built for the people running the community.

Coral is an activation tool for wallet teams and community leads. The number that matters is cost per activated wallet — and every step of the funnel is measured so you can see exactly where newcomers succeed, stall, or need a person.

  • Funnel you can read Conversion per step, time-in-step, stall queue, escalation rate — from the event log, not from vibes.
  • Escalations with context Your ops group receives the user's step, history, and trigger — volunteers pick up a briefing, not a mystery.
  • Your number, your data Runs on your own WhatsApp identity via the open-source NanoClaw runtime. The event log lives on your storage.
  • Open source, MIT The state machine, safety policy, and verification layer are all inspectable — as they should be, in this space.
